# Allocation evaluation

Generated by `python -m app.allocation_eval`. All four strategies replay the **same simulated
week** (1262 bookings, 150 workshops) against the simulator's own acceptance
model. Absolute numbers describe the simulator; the ordering of strategies and the trade-offs are
what carry over. Real results come from the pilot (`docs/pilot-report.md`).

## Tuned weights

Grid search over 243 weight combinations on the week before the reporting week
(objective: mean ETA relative to nearest-only + share not assigned within 60 s + Gini of jobs + no-match rate).

| eta | rating | load | accept | fairness |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.500 | 0.200 | 0.100 | 0.100 | 0.100 |

Starting values were 0.35 / 0.20 / 0.15 / 0.20 / 0.10. The chosen weights are written to
`artifacts/allocation_weights.json` and served at `GET /allocation/weights`.

## Reading this

- The ServiGo 1.0 rule (`highest_rating`) ignores distance: mean ETA 26.9 min against 4.2 for nearest-only, and a Gini of jobs of 0.89 against 0.28 for the weighted rule.
- `weighted_greedy` costs +0.8 min of mean ETA relative to nearest-only, and changes the Gini of jobs by -0.126 and offer acceptance by +3.0 points.
- At peak, batch assignment moved mean ETA by -0.7 min and the no-match rate by +1.9 points versus greedy; share assigned within 60 s moved by -8.8 points (it includes the wait for the 30 s window).
- Utilisation is shown only for the normal week; with arrivals compressed, jobs still take real time so the ratio means nothing.
